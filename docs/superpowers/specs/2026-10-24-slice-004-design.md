## Selected architecture

These are proposed engineering choices by the author, not existing implementations.

1. **One executor.** Replace acquisition-only priority with an episode-owned stack
   of value execution frames in the existing `TurnEngine`. A frame has a stable ID,
   parent, Sequence/PlayerOrdered mode, sourced pending work and a typed continuation.
   Child frames finish before their parent becomes actionable. Existing card/board
   arrays retain PlayerOrdered semantics. Explicit Sequence effects opt into strict
   order. Reveal's root frame permits purchases between siblings; children do not.
2. **Explicit roles.** Context distinguishes initiator, chooser, payer and recipient,
   with typed card/definition/space provenance and parent-frame identity. Targets
   belong to the specific operation, such as a control space or chosen Faction.
   Preserve existing OptionalPayment/ForPlayer defaults; explicit overrides express
   foreign choices whose recipient or payer remains another player. No untyped bag,
   copy/Graft participants or hypothetical expansion state is added.
3. **Continuation, not empty-queue inference.** Typed destinations cover Agent
   completion, Reveal root, acquisition return, Combat opportunity, Conflict reward,
   post-win window, Round Start and Endgame. `NextPlayer` runs only for the Agent/
   Reveal continuation that owns it. A Combat child never advances ordinary turns.
4. **Phase-aware windows.** Phases are BeforeRoundStart, RoundStart, PlayerTurns,
   Combat, Makers, Recall, Endgame and Completed. Deterministic administration drains
   internally. A window records kind, ordered eligible seats, cursor, opportunity
   play count, pass evidence, parent continuation and suspended child frame. Normal
   `CurrentPlayerId` is null outside PlayerTurns; `DecisionOwnerId` is authoritative.
5. **Small typed lifecycle definitions.** Extend `TurnRuleSet` with an optional
   `LifecycleRuleSet` containing sourced Conflict/Intrigue/Faction definitions and
   original participation/cleanup policies. Null retains the bounded turn-preview
   contract, stopping at Combat with a support boundary rather than completion.
   A lifecycle-enabled scenario must pass definition/deck support checks before it
   starts. Partial official registries cannot silently prune the physical decks.
6. **Outcome and rewards.** Freeze an explicit outcome after Combat priority closes.
   Outcome records include positive-strength ranks, tie groups, unique winner or
   none, reward tier per player and exclusions. Reward progress uses the same frames.
   The unique winner may order their remaining reward effects and supported post-win
   plays before closing their reward opportunity. Lower-place rewards follow; no
   cleanup until all rewards and post-win work finish. Sourced exceptions beyond
   this selected scope remain gated.
7. **Minimal lifetime state.** Track acquired Swordmaster and the Mentat's owner,
   placement kind and protected-through round. Conflict-awarded Mentat survives the
   current Recall, is usable next round, and returns at the following Recall.
   Preserve existing once/game space usage. No universal modifier/event framework.
8. **Dune result, existing runner.** Add a Dune-specific terminal DTO and result
   calculator. A development-only fixture adapter supplies complete Reset/Step/
   result semantics to existing `EpisodeRunner`; `TurnEngine` does not implement
   `IGame`, and Toy remains separate. No new runner or environment framework.

Alternatives rejected: more acquisition-depth flags, a separate Combat resolver,
a workflow/scripting library, blanket rollback/cloning per action and a new episode
runner. They duplicate working contracts or add cost without solving a demonstrated
need. BCL records/collections, System.Text.Json, xUnit and BenchmarkDotNet suffice;
no dependency change is planned.
Official [Microsoft Learn polymorphism guidance](https://learn.microsoft.com/dotnet/standard/serialization/system-text-json/polymorphism)
supports retaining explicit derived-type allowlists and shared serializer options.

## Safety, privacy and replay

Validate immutable graph shape/cycles/source requirements once and retain per-box
support diagnostics; unsupported catalog entries do not become constructor crashes.
At an action boundary validate actor, phase, selected branch, affordability, finite
stock, permanent upgrades, targets and continuation before payment/card/RNG/history
mutation. Optional unaffordable branches may still be declined; unsupported mechanics
cannot be hidden behind optionality. Existing mandatory acquisition stock guarantees
must survive nested frames and interleaved purchases. Invariant exceptions remain
defects with no general rollback guarantee.

Implemented P1 dynamic prevalidation conservatively gates unresolved correlated mandatory choices for stock,
resource capacity, permanent upgrades and funding. Only current funds, selected debits and earlier uninterrupted
Sequence cash gains can prove payment; later/suspended income and unordered peer funding are not assumed. Optional
decline and proven free alternatives remain supported. These are explicit Unsupported composite contracts rather
than a general feasibility solver; source-enabled lifecycle definitions must remain inside the tested shapes.

Trusted snapshots include every frame, cursor, role binding, window, pass, outcome,
reward item, round-lifetime field and Endgame progress. Deliberately introduce
P1 v2 formats, followed by P2 `dune-turn-replay-v3`, `dune-turn-snapshot-v3`,
`dune-setup-snapshot-v3` and executable preview/support revisions v3. Future incompatible
serialized state/semantics require a deliberate subsequent revision. Reject v1 at the header boundary; no silent
reinterpretation or compatibility emulator. Preserve Toy replay and configuration
identity formats; change composition/content fingerprints only for actual metadata
changes. Terminal result replay identity uses a canonical replay-header/action digest
independent of the final result hash, avoiding a circular hash definition.

Players see only detached public phase/window summaries, their private cards and
their current legal choices. No hidden opponent card count conditions may change
another player's action set; evaluate such scoring conditions inside resolution.
No physical allocation IDs, hidden deck identity/order, RNG or trusted frames in
observations, support messages or ordinary logs. Unsupported hidden-deck diagnostics
must be generic and independent of the current top card/hand. All mutable state is
episode-local, including pass/usage/reward state.
